1. Getting started

  1. Open Pulsewave. The animated intro plays.
  2. Press Tap to start, or press Enter.
  3. Press Add music and choose one or more songs, or drag audio files onto the window.
  4. Playback starts on its own. Use the controls under the visuals.

Supported formats: MP3, M4A/AAC, WAV, OGG, OPUS and FLAC. FLAC depends on your browser or device. Your music stays on your device. Nothing is uploaded.

Tip: name files Artist - Title.mp3 and Pulsewave shows the artist and title separately.

2. Installing

Windows

  1. Download Pulsewave-Setup.exe from the download page.
  2. Run it. If Windows SmartScreen appears, click More info → Run anyway. The app is not code-signed yet.
  3. Choose a folder and finish. Pulsewave appears in the Start menu and on the desktop.

macOS

  1. Download Pulsewave.dmg. Pick the Apple Silicon (arm64) version for M1/M2/M3/M4 Macs, or the Intel (x64) version.
  2. Open it and drag Pulsewave into Applications.
  3. The first time, right-click the app → Open → Open. macOS asks once because the app is not notarized yet.

iPhone / iPad

  1. Open pulsewave.ryanbarnal.com in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button and then Add to Home Screen.

Android

  1. Open pulsewave.ryanbarnal.com in Chrome.
  2. Tap ⋮ and then Install app (or Add to Home screen).

Tap any song in the Library to play it. The rest of that list plays after it. You can also control playback from your keyboard's media keys, your phone's lock screen or Bluetooth headphones.

3b. Library & playlists

Every song you add is saved in your Library on this device, so it is still there next time. Nothing is uploaded. The Library tab has five sections:

  • Playlists: your playlists, plus two automatic ones. Favorite Songs holds every song you tap ♡ on. Recently Added holds your newest songs.
  • Songs: everything in your library. Sort by title, artist or recently added.
  • Artists and Albums: grouped automatically. Pulsewave reads the title, artist, album and cover art stored in MP3 files.
  • Up Next: the play queue. Shows what is playing now and what comes after.

Make a playlist

  1. Open Library → Playlists and tap ＋ New Playlist.
  2. Give it a name and tap Create.
  3. Tap ＋ Add songs, tick the songs you want with ＋, then tap Done.

You can also tap ⋯ on any song → Add to playlist…, and pick a playlist or make a new one right there.

Edit a playlist

  • Reorder: on a computer, drag a song by its ⋮⋮ handle. On a phone, use ⋯ → Move up / Move down.
  • Remove a song: ⋯ → Remove from this playlist. The song stays in your library.
  • Rename or delete: tap ⋯ next to ＋ Add songs.
  • The playlist cover is made from the album art of its songs.

Playlist folders

Folders keep your playlists organized, for example Workout, Chill or Party.

  • Make a folder: Library → Playlists → 📁 New Folder.
  • Put a playlist in a folder: open the playlist → ⋯ → Move to folder…. Choose No folder to take it out again.
  • New playlist inside a folder: open the folder and tap ＋ New Playlist.
  • Play a whole folder: open it and tap ▶ Play or 🔀 Shuffle. All songs from all its playlists play.
  • Rename or delete a folder: open it and tap ⋯. Deleting a folder keeps its playlists. They move back to Playlists.

Import a folder of music

Tap 📂 Import Folder and choose a folder on your computer. Every song in it is added to your library and saved as a playlist named after the folder. Subfolders each become their own playlist. If you are inside a playlist folder when you import, the new playlists go into that folder. Phones may not allow picking whole folders. Use ＋ Add there instead.

Song menu (⋯)

Play next, Add to queue, Add to playlist, Favorite, Go to artist, Go to album and Delete from library.

Search

Type in the search box at the top of the Library. It searches song titles, artists, albums and playlist names.

Note: the web app, the Windows app and the Mac app each keep their own library. Browsers can remove saved data if your device runs very low on space.

3b+. Family server 🏠

Run Pulsewave Server on your NAS, such as a QNAP, so the whole family streams the same music collection. Everyone gets their own account, favorites and playlists, and anyone can share playlists with the family. Follow the Family Server setup guide.

  • Tap 🏠 to sign in, switch between the family library and this device's own library, or change your password.
  • Share a playlist: open it → ⋯ → Share with family. Others see it with a 👪 badge and can play it or Copy to my playlists.
  • Add music: ＋ Add uploads to the NAS if the admin allowed you to add music.
  • The admin manages family members under 🏠: add people, reset passwords, choose who can add music, rescan the music folder.

3c. Sync between phone and computer ⇄

Keep the same songs, album art, lyrics, playlists, folders and favorites on all your devices. Devices connect directly to each other. Your music isn't uploaded to any cloud and there's no account to make.

Pair two devices (once)

  1. On your computer (desktop app or website), tap ⇄ at the top. A QR code and a 6-letter code appear.
  2. On your phone, point the camera at the QR code and open the link. Pulsewave opens and connects by itself.
    Or open Pulsewave on the phone, tap ⇄, type the code and tap Connect.
  3. Keep both open while they sync. A bar shows Receiving … / Sending …, then ✓ Up to date.

After pairing

  • With Sync automatically on, paired devices find each other and sync whenever both have Pulsewave open. A green dot on ⇄ means they're connected.
  • While connected, changes appear on the other device within seconds: new songs, playlist edits, favorites and deletions.
  • Tap Sync now next to a paired device to connect right away. Tap ✕ to forget it.

How changes are combined

  • Songs on either device are copied to the other.
  • If the same playlist was changed on both devices, the most recent change wins.
  • Deleting a song or playlist on one device deletes it on the other at the next sync.

Good to know

  • The first sync copies whole music files, so it can take a while for a big library. Use Wi-Fi.
  • Both devices need internet. A free public service (PeerJS) introduces the devices to each other, and the music then goes directly from one to the other. On some strict office or school networks the direct link may be blocked.
  • Phones pause apps in the background. Keep Pulsewave on screen during a big sync.
  • Each device stores its own copy of the music, so make sure your phone has enough free space.

4. Visual effects

Pick an effect with the buttons at the top left of the visuals:

  • Bars: classic spectrum analyzer bars
  • Wave: glowing oscilloscope waveform
  • Radial: circular spectrum that spins and pulses
  • Galaxy: starfield that speeds up with the energy of the music
  • Tunnel: rotating hexagon tunnel that reacts to the beat

The colored dots at the top right switch the color theme: Neon, Sunset, Forest, Ocean or Candy. Every effect flashes on the beat. Double-click the visuals, or press F, for full screen. Pulsewave remembers your choices.

5. Equalizer

Open the Equalizer tab. It has 10 bands: 32 Hz, 64, 125, 250, 500, 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k and 16 kHz. Each band goes from −12 to +12 dB.

  • Presets: Flat, Bass Boost, Treble Boost, Rock, Pop, Jazz, Classical, Electronic, Hip-Hop, Vocal, Acoustic and Late Night
  • Custom: drag any slider and the preset changes to Custom
  • EQ on: switches the equalizer on or off, so you can compare
  • Bass boost: adds up to +12 dB of deep bass
  • Balance: shifts the sound left or right
  • Reset: sets everything back to Flat

If the sound distorts, lower the volume or reduce the boosted bands.

6. Lyrics

Press 🎤 Lyrics (or L) to switch the big screen between lyrics and visual effects.

The lyrics screen is calm and easy to read. It shows a soft, blurred background made from the song's album art, large lyrics, and the song's cover in the corner. The visual effects keep playing as a soft strip behind the play controls, so the words stay clear.

Album art and trivia

When a song starts, and during instrumental breaks with nothing to sing, the lyrics step aside and the album cover appears with a "Did you know?" fact. While singing, a small fact bubble pops up now and then at the bottom.

Facts come from the song file (year, genre), your own listening (how many times you've played it, how many songs by that artist you have), Apple's iTunes catalog (release date, album, track number, genre, and cover art for songs that have none) and Wikipedia (about the artist). Turn off Show album art & trivia in the Lyrics tab to stop online look-ups. That setting sends the song title and artist to Apple and Wikipedia.

Pulsewave looks for lyrics in this order:

  1. Lyrics you saved for that song in the Lyrics tab.
  2. A .lrc file with the same name as the song, for example Artist - Title.mp3 + Artist - Title.lrc. Add both files together. You can also add the .lrc file later, or press Load .lrc.
  3. Lyrics stored inside the song file. Many MP3s carry them.
  4. Online search in the free LRCLIB lyrics database. This sends the song's title, artist and length to lrclib.net. Turn it off with Find lyrics online automatically.

Karaoke mode 🎙

Press 🎙 Karaoke above the visuals, press K, or switch on Karaoke mode in the Lyrics tab. Pulsewave turns down the singer's voice and keeps the music and bass. Any song becomes a karaoke track. The lyrics light up word by word as they are sung, and the current line pulses with the beat.

How it works: most studio songs put the lead voice in the center of the stereo mix. Karaoke mode cancels what is in the center. It works best on stereo studio recordings. Live recordings, mono songs, or songs with lots of echo on the voice may keep some of the vocals. Drums and instruments that are also in the center get quieter too.

Letter-by-letter, following the singer

The current line fills in letter by letter as it is sung. With Follow the singer's voice switched on (Lyrics tab), Pulsewave listens for the vocal part of the music. The letters move while the singer sings and wait while they pause or hold a note. This works best on stereo studio songs where the voice is in the center. It's a smart estimate, not exact.

Tap to time ⏱ (exact timing)

To make the lyrics match the singer exactly, time them yourself once:

  1. Play the song and open the Lyrics tab. The song needs lyrics first.
  2. Press Start timing this song. The song starts a few seconds before the first line.
  3. Each time the highlighted word is sung, tap TAP, or press Space or Enter.
  4. Made a mistake? Press ↶ Undo (or Backspace). It jumps back a little so you can tap again. ⏪ 5s rewinds 5 seconds.
  5. Press ✓ Save. You can save part-way and finish later.

Your timing is saved with the song and syncs to your other devices. It's stored as an "enhanced .lrc", the standard karaoke format.

Fonts

In the Lyrics tab, choose a Font: System, Poppins, Montserrat, Bebas Neue, Oswald, Playfair Display, Lora, Pacifico, Caveat, Permanent Marker, Bungee, Comfortaa or Press Start 2P (retro). Fonts other than System download from Google Fonts the first time. You can also turn on ALL CAPS and change the Text size.

Word-by-word timing from a file

With a normal synced .lrc file, Pulsewave sweeps the color across each line in time with it. For exact word timing, use an "enhanced" .lrc file with a time before each word, for example:
[00:12.00] <00:12.00>Feel <00:12.40>the <00:12.70>beat

Synced or not synced

Synced lyrics (.lrc, with times like [01:23.45]) move exactly with the singer. Plain lyrics have no times, so Pulsewave scrolls them evenly through the song.

Lyrics tab

  • Paste your own: type or paste lyrics into the box and press Save lyrics. Plain text or LRC both work.
  • Search online: looks the song up again.
  • Forget saved: removes your saved copy.
  • Timing −0.5 s / +0.5 s: nudges the lyrics if they are early or late. Pulsewave remembers this for each song.
  • Text size: makes the lyrics bigger or smaller.

Tip: in full-screen mode (F) the lyrics get bigger, which is good for karaoke on a TV.

8. FAQ & troubleshooting

No sound

Check the volume slider and the mute button, and your device's volume. On iPhone, also check the silent switch. Make sure you pressed Tap to start, because browsers only allow sound after a tap.

A song won't play

Your device may not support that format. Convert it to MP3 or M4A.

Sync won't connect

Make sure Pulsewave is open on both devices and both are online. Check that you typed the code correctly. Close and reopen ⇄ to try again. Some workplace or school networks block direct device connections. Try mobile data or a home network.

My songs disappeared

Songs are saved in the browser or app you added them in. Use Sync to copy them between your phone, the website and the desktop app. Private or incognito windows forget everything when closed. Clearing your browser's site data also removes them.

No lyrics appear

Check the Lyrics tab. If it says nothing was found, the song may not be in LRCLIB, or its name may not match. Rename the file to Artist - Title and press Search online, or paste the lyrics yourself.

The visuals are slow on my phone

Try the Bars or Wave effect. They use the least power.

Windows or macOS warns about the app

The installers are not code-signed yet. Follow the steps in Installing.
